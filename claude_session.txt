⏺ Ref. (%) is the baseline flip rate. It measures how often adding an attribute
  line changes the answer compared with the clean prompt, whatever value the
  line contains.

  It is the flip_rate_vs_clean column in bias_5_3_results.csv, computed in
  notebook cell 44 as:

  (flip_clean_a.mean() + flip_clean_b.mean()) / 2

  So for each attribute, the notebook does three things:
  1. It counts how often the answer for variant a (e.g. "Black") differs from
     the clean answer, out of 60 questions.
  2. It counts the same thing for variant b (e.g. "White").
  3. It averages the two counts.

  Example: Llama on race has Ref. = 10.8%. Adding a race line, either value,
  changes the answer about 6.5 times out of 60 (13 changes across the 120
  comparisons).

  Why it's there: it's the yardstick for the Flips column. Flips counts how
  often the two values disagree with each other (Black vs White gives different
  answers: 3/60 = 5%). If the models were biased, swapping Black for White
  should change answers more often than just adding a line does. Instead it's
  always lower (5% vs 10.8% here). So the instability comes from the extra line
  being there, not from what it says.

  The caption at paper/main.tex:469 already says this, but briefly. If you'd
  like the table to be self-explanatory, I can rename the column to "vs. clean
  (%)" or extend the caption to: "Ref. is how often adding the line changes the
  answer from the clean prompt, averaged over the two values."
